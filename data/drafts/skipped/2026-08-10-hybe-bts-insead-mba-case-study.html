HYBE and BTS have been featured as a case study at a prestigious overseas MBA program. According to the K-pop industry on August 10, the Blue Ocean Strategy Institute at INSEAD, a renowned French business school, recently published a case study titled 'HYBE: Building Growth Beyond BTS,' analyzing HYBE's successful strategies.

The study was authored by Senior Research Fellow Koo Young and Professors W. Chan Kim and Renée Mauborgne. The researchers defined HYBE as a global entertainment company that has built a mid-to-long-term growth formula, analyzing its strategies from a business management perspective. They concluded that HYBE achieved explosive growth through a Blue Ocean strategy encompassing BTS's debut, the construction of the fan platform Weverse, and the expansion of the K-pop ecosystem — laying the groundwork for diversification across genres, regions, and business areas.

The researchers highlighted BTS's path to success, stating that 'HYBE, as a mid-sized agency, went beyond competing within the K-pop market through BTS to create a new customer base in the global music industry.' Key factors cited for the team's success included member-written songs, narrative-driven music, digital platform-based communication that attracted new customers beyond the existing K-pop fandom, and a virtuous cycle linking content, community, and commerce built on the relationship with the fandom 'ARMY.'

However, the researchers noted that BTS's enormous success left the challenge of over-reliance on a single group. The case study also introduced HYBE Chairman Bang Si-hyuk's 'K-pop crisis theory,' which he presented at the Gwanhun Forum in 2023. The researchers analyzed that HYBE responded to this challenge through a multi-label strategy and glocalization. HYBE built a multi-label system that combines acquiring existing labels with developing new artists rather than uniform production, enabling each label to grow rapidly while maintaining its independence. The researchers noted this approach is also effective in spreading risk concentrated on specific artists across multiple genres, generations, and markets.

The researchers also identified global localization — building local business foundations at overseas bases — as HYBE's second growth strategy. They described how HYBE transplanted its 'K-pop methodology' covering production and development to each base, operating intellectual property (IP) tailored to the cultural characteristics of each market. Through this approach, HYBE has produced local artists including the Korean-American co-produced girl group KATSEYE, Japan's Aoен, and Latin America's Santos Bravos.

The researchers additionally noted HYBE's 'HYBE 2.0,' a restructuring of its business model in 2024 from the existing labels, solutions, and platform structure toward music, platform, and technology-based future growth. They stated, 'Through the restructuring, HYBE aims to reduce dependence on a small number of superstars, strengthen the super-fan economy, and expand its business across multiple regions and music genres,' while adding that 'whether HYBE 2.0 represents a genuine strategic renewal or merely a reframing of the company's growth narrative remains uncertain.' This marks the first time INSEAD has featured a K-pop company as a case study, and the research will be used as course material at approximately 3,000 universities worldwide.
