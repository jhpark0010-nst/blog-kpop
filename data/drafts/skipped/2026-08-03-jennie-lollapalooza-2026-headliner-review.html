BLACKPINK's Jennie made history on August 1, 2026, becoming the first K-pop female solo artist ever to headline Lollapalooza, taking the main stage at Grant Park in Chicago, Illinois. However, her performance drew sharply divided reactions, with criticism directed at her early stage presence and a setlist built heavily around unreleased tracks.

American outlet the Chicago Sun-Times reviewed the show, writing that 'unlike a full BLACKPINK stage, she showed difficulty commanding the large venue on her own.' The publication noted that the numerous dancers and massive stage production actually seemed to swallow her up, and that an imbalance in the sound mix left some vocals sounding flat.

The review was not without praise. The Chicago Sun-Times acknowledged that during performances of signature songs like 'Mantra,' 'XtraL,' and 'Like Jennie,' Jennie proved why she was chosen as a headliner. The analysis noted that her distinctive presence revived as the show progressed into its second half, with intense choreography working in sync with the stage production.

Harsher words were reserved for the 'Dracula' stage performed with musician Kevin Parker. The outlet described it as sounding 'like a glamorously packaged karaoke session,' citing a disconnect between Jennie's live vocals and the recorded backing track. The consecutive placement of unfamiliar unreleased songs — including 'Heaven,' 'Rock It Down,' and 'Sweet Tooth' — was also flagged as a weakness. While fans near the front responded enthusiastically, general audience engagement reportedly dropped and some attendees left.

Positive voices were also plentiful. The BLACKPINK fan community was filled with firsthand accounts such as 'the stage production was outstanding and Jennie's energy and presence shone through' and 'the wait in the rain and mud was worth it.' Some audience members also testified that her voice came through far more clearly in person than it did on the live stream.

On the night, Jennie stood at the center of the world's largest music festival not as a member of BLACKPINK but as a solo artist — yet the verdict on her ability to lead a major stage alone was decidedly split.
