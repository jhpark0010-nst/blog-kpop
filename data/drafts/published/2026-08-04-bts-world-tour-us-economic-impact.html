BTS's resumed world tour is generating a massive economic ripple effect across North America, according to international media reports. British newspaper The Guardian published a feature titled 'BTS are back: return of K-pop superstars sparks US economic boom,' spotlighting how BTS — returning after a four-year hiatus — is driving an enormous economic boom across the United States.

The Guardian reported that 16 concerts scheduled across 7 North American cities in August and September, combined with HYBE's 'THE CITY' project, are spreading what it calls 'BTSnomics.' Regarding BTS's four consecutive sold-out shows in Las Vegas in May, the outlet estimated that the events generated approximately $340 million (around 450 billion Korean won) in economic impact, benefiting everything from large hotels to small cafes in Chinatown.

The MetLife Stadium concerts in New Jersey on August 1 and 2 drew a combined audience of approximately 157,000 fans, with large numbers of ARMY (the group's fandom) also flooding Manhattan's Koreatown. Michael Mariano, head of economic development at Tourism Economics, told The Guardian: 'Looking at BTS fans' average spending alone — accommodation, food and drink, flights, and other related expenditures beyond ticket prices — the impact could rival or even exceed that of the FIFA World Cup.'

The results of BTS's tour are also reflected in HYBE's financial performance. HYBE, the parent company of Big Hit Music, recorded quarterly revenue of 1.45 trillion Korean won in Q2 of this year — the first time any entertainment company has surpassed 1 trillion won in a single quarter, and the highest quarterly result in HYBE's history.

The Guardian also highlighted the impact of the 'BTS THE CITY ARIRANG' project, which draws on BTS album titles and connects cultural arts with local commerce. The Korean Cultural Center New York was transformed into a K-beauty experience zone and ARMY Bomb (light stick) customization area, while Arte Museum presented a media art exhibition with immersive visuals and sound. Korean restaurants and pop-up stores in New York drew strong responses by featuring member-favorite Korean dishes such as samgyeopsal (grilled pork belly), jjajangmyeon (black bean noodles), and bingsu (shaved ice dessert).

Yoon Bora, Visual Arts Officer at the Korean Cultural Center New York, told The Guardian: 'K-pop is serving as a massive gateway that leads people to discover not only the Korean language, but Korean beauty, food, technology, tourism, and culture as a whole.' BTS's journey — demonstrating the power of music to connect cities and cultures in the world's largest pop market — is acting as a powerful momentum driving the entire global industry.
