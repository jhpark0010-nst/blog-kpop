BTS announced on July 29, 2026 that they will not submit their music to the 69th Grammy Awards, scheduled for February next year. The group made the announcement through their official social media channels, stating: 'We have decided not to submit to the Grammys this year.'

In their statement, BTS said, 'We hope music can be heard and loved as music itself, rather than being categorized by region or language.' They also expressed gratitude to their fanbase ARMY and all their supporters.

The decision comes despite the massive global success of BTS's fifth studio album ARIRANG, released in March 2026, which topped both the Billboard main singles and albums charts in the United States. The move is widely interpreted as a protest against the Recording Academy's announcement last month that it would introduce a new category called 'Best Asian Pop Music Performance,' covering K-pop, J-pop, and C-pop.

The new 'Best Asian Pop Music Performance' category was created to honor excellence in Asian pop music — defined as music that meaningfully uses one or more Asian languages and is made by or recognized among artists of Asian origin. The Recording Academy stated the category was established in recognition of the growing influence of Asian pop on the global music industry.

However, critics in the music industry pointed out that grouping the popular music of Korea, China, and Japan — which share little musical common ground — under the single regional label of 'Asia' reflects a Western-centric perspective. There were also concerns that the new category would effectively confine K-pop, which has been growing in influence in the U.S. and global markets, away from the major General Fields categories. While the new category superficially raises K-pop's chances of winning a Grammy, it was seen as making meaningful recognition in the main pop categories far less likely. BTS's own statement — referencing music being 'categorized by region or language' — lent weight to this interpretation.

The Grammy Awards, held annually since 1959 by the Recording Academy, has long faced criticism for being conservative and exclusionary toward non-white artists compared to other U.S. music awards. The Academy's membership, largely composed of traditional figures from the American mainstream music industry, has been criticized for being reluctant to embrace new choices. BTS first appeared at the Grammys in 2019 as presenters for the Best R&B Album category, and in the following year became the first K-pop act to perform on the Grammy stage, joining Lil Nas X. They were nominated for Best Pop Duo/Group Performance for three consecutive years starting in 2021, but did not win. At a 2021 press conference, BTS had expressed strong motivation to win a Grammy, saying 'There is no tree that won't fall after being struck ten times,' but after completing their military service and staging a successful comeback, they have now indicated they will not seek to prove themselves through the Grammy trophy.

Music critic Im Hee-yoon commented on BTS's decision: 'This is a bold move against the Grammy Awards, which is considered the highest authority in the music world. BTS has already become an artist that doesn't need to win a Grammy.' He added, 'BTS and ARMY have together changed many things over the years — setting trends and overcoming opposition — and that has been a great force in breaking into the U.S. market. This decision likely comes from that same momentum.'
