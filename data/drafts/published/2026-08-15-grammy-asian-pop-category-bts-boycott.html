About two weeks after BTS announced a boycott, the Grammy Awards signaled it may revise the newly created Best Asian Pop Music Performance category. Billboard reported on August 14 (local time) that Recording Academy CEO Harvey Mason Jr. issued a statement addressing the controversy surrounding the category.

In the statement, Mason said, 'Regardless of our intentions, it has become clear that some artists feel the [Asian Pop] category does not represent the music they have worked so hard to create,' adding, 'As a musician myself, I take that to heart.'

He continued, 'Over the past six years, the Recording Academy has shown that it can move decisively and swiftly to meet the needs of the music community,' and emphasized, 'We will work to keep pace with that progress and ensure that musicians truly feel respected.'

Mason also said the Academy would consider overhauling the process by which new award categories are developed. He added, 'Now that the Grammy Awards has surpassed 100 categories, it is time to think about how we can evolve our efforts.' Based on these remarks, Billboard reported that the Recording Academy may unusually restructure the Asian Pop category as early as next year's ceremony.

The Recording Academy is currently in contact with music industry figures across Asia. The Academy stated that Mason and other organizational leaders have met with musicians, labels, and managers representing K-pop, J-pop (Japanese popular music), C-pop (Chinese popular music), and India, including HYBE. The Academy has also convened its full board of directors and nominations committee, and is conducting separate advisory work on the Asian Pop category. It is understood that the Academy has set a goal of respectfully and accurately honoring the music from the very first time the award is presented.

In June, the Grammy Awards announced the creation of the 'Best Asian Pop Music Performance' category, separating music from Korea, China, Japan, and other parts of Asia. This drew criticism that the Grammys were merely paying lip service to Asian music despite K-pop's global popularity, rather than recognizing it in the main categories. BTS then declared a boycott on July 29, citing the creation of the Asian Pop category as the issue. The group stated, 'We have decided not to submit our music to the Grammys this year,' and expressed their hope that 'music can be heard and loved as music itself, rather than being divided by region or language.'
