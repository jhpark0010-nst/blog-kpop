BTS announced a boycott of the Grammy Awards on July 29, 2026, turning the spotlight onto who will claim the first-ever trophy in the newly created Best Asian Pop Music Performance category at next year's ceremony. The group performed at the 2026 FIFA North America World Cup final halftime show at MetLife Stadium in New Jersey on July 19.

HYBE and other major K-pop agencies have responded cautiously, noting that the submission period has not yet closed. Experts predict that while artists from other Asian countries could be contenders, a well-known K-pop star is most likely to take home the award. The deadline for Grammy submissions is August 21 (local time).

BTS stated via social media that day: 'We hope music can be heard and loved as music itself, rather than being divided by region or language.' The statement served as an indirect criticism of the new Asian Pop category and a declaration of their intent to boycott the Grammys. To date, no K-pop artist has won a Grammy trophy, with the sole exception being the animated film soundtrack 'GOLDEN' from 'K-Pop Demon Hunters,' which won Best Song Written for Visual Media earlier this year in February.

HYBE said the decision on whether to submit entries for its other artists rests with those artists themselves. 'HYBE operates a multi-label system and respects the wishes of each label and artist,' a HYBE representative said. One music agency official commented: 'The creation of the Asian Pop category seems to limit the very playing field that K-pop stars can compete on. Younger artists might find BTS's decision admirable, but it is also a decision that only BTS could make.' The official added: 'It is difficult for other agencies to raise their voices toward the Grammys. This situation should be seen as a story between the Grammys and BTS, rather than a broader issue between the Grammys and K-pop.'

Music critic Lim Hee-yoon said: 'Excluding BTS, BLACKPINK and Stray Kids — who have sold out stadium concerts and headlined major music festivals — seem to have the best chances. Winning a Grammy also requires a network within the United States, so I think it will likely come down to artists signed to major agencies.' The critic also noted that Philippine girl group BINI performed at Coachella and ranked among the top acts in viewership, but added: 'Realistically, I think the chances of a non-K-pop Asian artist winning are low.'

Professor Shin Hyun-joon of Seoul's Sungkonghoe University offered a broader analysis: 'It is worth considering why the Grammys created an Asian Pop category rather than a K-pop category. Among genres that are popular but not mainstream in the Western world, the top three would be reggaeton, Afrobeats, and K-pop. Reggaeton has a regional base spanning Panama and Puerto Rico, and Afrobeats spans Nigeria and Ghana, but K-pop is associated with only one specific country. The Grammys may have found that uncomfortable.' He continued: 'K-pop also features many songs written by Scandinavian composers and members of various nationalities including Chinese and Japanese. There is a question of whether K-pop can truly be called exclusively Korean. On top of that, Japan is the world's second-largest music market, and China's market is rising rapidly — those factors were likely considered as well.'

Music critic Lim Jin-mo said: 'The Grammys still have not shed their traditional conservatism at all. The reality is that they exclude K-pop, which has achieved great popularity in markets around the world, and give awards according to their own artistic framework. BTS's declaration reads as a move to break free from the Grammys and pursue a path of music unfettered by the prospect of winning awards.' Support also came from fellow artists: Epik High's Tablo quoted RM's Grammy boycott post on social media and wrote 'ARIRANG > GRAMMYS' in a show of solidarity.
